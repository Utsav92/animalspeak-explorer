Bioacoustic caption corpus

AnimalSpeak Explorer

AnimalSpeak (David Robinson, HuggingFace) pairs 894,256 wildlife field recordings with natural-language captions, pooled from Xeno-canto, iNaturalist, the Animal Sound Archive and the Watkins Marine Mammal database. The dataset itself ships no waveform-derived acoustics, only text, so the first plot below measures the caption corpus: how often a species was recorded, by how many distinct people, and how its captions are written. For the second plot we went further and pulled the actual audio — real pitch, loudness and spectral shape measured from the waveform of a sampled recording per species.

Acoustic fingerprints

Real audio, not captions. For 39 species — birds, mammals (including six whales and dolphins), amphibians, insects and a reptile — we downloaded a field recording each, ran it through a 300 Hz highpass filter and an FFT, and measured these parameters straight from the waveform. AnimalSpeak has no fish recordings: Xeno-canto and iNaturalist skew almost entirely to birds, and the dataset's only marine audio is whales and dolphins, not fish. Most mammal clips here are AnimalSpeak's own iNaturalist-sourced recordings; six cetaceans (Killer Whale, Common Dolphin, Long-finned Pilot Whale, Risso's Dolphin, Striped Dolphin, Bottlenose Dolphin) use public-domain NOAA Fisheries recordings instead, because AnimalSpeak's own source for them — the Watkins Marine Mammal archive — is hosted on a server that has been down for maintenance since this page was built.

Corpus plot computed with pandas from the published caption/metadata parquet export; recordist and source counts reflect attribution present in the metadata, not ground truth. Acoustic clips are short excerpts (≤6s, one per species), mostly re-encoded from the dataset's own linked Xeno-canto, iNaturalist and Tierstimmenarchiv recordings and credited to their original recordists via the source URL in the metadata. Six whale/dolphin clips (marked in their spotlight card) come from NOAA Fisheries' public-domain ocean sound library instead, standing in for AnimalSpeak's own Watkins-sourced recordings of those species while that archive's host is offline. Every clip is used here for analysis and playback only.
